Getting started 4 answers

How does an engagement begin?

With a fixed-fee operating diagnostic. We spend two weeks inside the business mapping how work actually moves — not how the org chart says it does — and come back with a written picture of where effort is going and what it would take to change that.

What do you need from us to start?

Access to the people who do the work, and read access to the systems that hold the information. No preparation, no pre-work, no pack to assemble. If you have to tidy up before we arrive, we are looking at the wrong thing.

How long before anything actually changes?

The diagnostic takes two weeks and usually surfaces immediate fixes worth making straight away. The designed operating layer is typically live by week ten.

Can we start with one area rather than the whole business?

Yes, and often that is the right call. One function done properly is a better proof than four done partially.

How we work 5 answers

Are you replacing our operations team?

No. We own the operating layer around your team — structure, standards, reporting and systems — so the people you already have stop spending their week holding it together manually.

Who is actually doing the work day to day?

A named operating lead who knows your business, supported by the specialists a given piece of work needs. You are not handed to a rotating pool, and you are not sold a partner and delivered a graduate.

How is the work governed?

A weekly operating report to leadership, a monthly review against the operating model, and a quarterly reset of priorities. Everything we run is visible to you in the same view we use.

What happens when something goes wrong?

It appears in the weekly report before you have to ask about it. Surfacing problems late is the failure mode we are hired to remove, so we do not get to practise it ourselves.

Do you work on site?

Where it helps. Operational work needs enough presence to see how things really run, and not so much that it becomes theatre.

Systems and data 4 answers

Do we have to change our systems?

Rarely. Most businesses do not have a tooling problem — they have an information problem. We connect what you already run into one operating view before recommending anything be replaced.

What if our tools are unusual or homegrown?

That is common in businesses that grew fast. If a system can export or expose its data, it can join the operating view. Where it cannot, we say so early rather than discovering it in month three.

Who owns the systems and the documentation?

You do, throughout. Every process we document, every integration we build and every report we run is yours, in your accounts, from the first day.

How do you handle our data?

We work inside your systems under your access controls wherever possible, with the narrowest permissions the work allows. Anything we hold is covered by the engagement agreement.

Commercials 4 answers

What does an engagement cost?

A fixed-fee diagnostic, then a monthly retainer sized to the operating layer we run. No percentages and no open-ended day rates. Indicative figures are on the engagement model section of the homepage.

How long is the commitment?

The diagnostic stands alone — you can stop there and keep everything it produced. Ongoing management is typically a twelve-month term, because operating change measured in weeks is not change.

What is included in the retainer?

The named operating lead, the operating rhythm, the reporting, and the maintenance of the systems and documentation we put in place. Substantial new build is scoped separately so it never quietly eats the retainer.

What happens if we want to stop?

You keep the documentation, the integrations and the operating view, and we hand over to whoever takes it on. An exit that leaves you worse off than before would make the whole proposition dishonest.

Whether we are a fit 4 answers

What size of business do you work with?

Typically fifty to three hundred people, where the founder-era way of running things has stopped scaling but a full internal operations function is not yet justified.

Which sectors do you work in?

Operations problems rhyme across sectors. The industries section of the homepage lists where structure tends to pay for itself fastest, but the pattern — ownership, process, visibility — is the same everywhere.

When are we not the right partner?

If you want advice rather than execution, or a report you can file. We take ownership of the operating layer, which only works if you actually want someone else running it.

Do you work with businesses that already have a COO?

Often. A good COO with no operating layer beneath them spends their week doing coordination instead of leadership. We build the layer so the role becomes what it was meant to be.

Outcomes 4 answers

How do you measure whether it worked?

Against the measures agreed in the diagnostic, reported weekly from the same operating view you can see at any time. Not against a satisfaction survey.

What changes first?

Visibility, almost always. Knowing where work actually stands is what makes every later decision cheaper, and it is usually available within days rather than months.

What if it does not work?

You will know early, because the reporting is weekly and open. If the operating model is wrong we change it; if we are the wrong partner we say so rather than running out the term.

Can you show us a client example?

Yes — the case study walks through a distribution group that replaced coordination effort with structure. Figures on it are marked as samples until the client story is published.
